The 3-minute hackathon pitch template

Fill in the blanks, rehearse it with a timer, and stop at 3:00. Judges see dozens of teams in a day; the ones that stay on time and show one working flow are the ones they remember.

The clock

0:00The problem, as one person's story. 30 seconds.

0:30Live demo, one flow. 90 seconds. This is most of your pitch.

2:00Impact. 30 seconds, one real number.

2:30Team and what's next. 30 seconds.

3:00Stop. Even if you have more to say.

Fill in the blanks

0:00Problem

Meet name, a who they are. Every day / week they the task, and it what goes wrong. That costs them time / money / a missed chance.

Use a real person you talked to, or the exact kind of person the problem statement names. Skip the team intro and the "so basically" setup.

0:30Live demo

So we built product name. Here's name doing the task now: step 1 → step 2 → the result they see.

One flow, clicked live. Narrate what the user sees, not your code. If something breaks, switch to your backup video without apologising for more than one line.

2:00Impact

The task goes from before to after. How many people like name deal with this where / how often.

One number you can point to a source for beats three you made up. If you don't have one, measure your own demo: "4 taps instead of 11".

2:30Team and what's next

Name built part, name designed part, and I part. Next, we'd one feature and test it with real users.

3:00Close

That's product name, so name never has to the pain again. Thank you.

Before you walk up

  • Backup demo video saved on the laptop and a phone
  • Demo data already loaded; you're logged in; notifications off
  • Screen mirroring tested on the venue's display, text zoomed in
  • One person drives the demo, one person talks
  • Timer running on a phone where the speaker can see it
  • Rehearsed out loud at least three times, under 3:00 each time

Questions judges usually ask

Prepare a one-line answer to each:

  • Who is the user, and did you talk to one?
  • What actually works right now, and what's mocked?
  • Why is this better than what they use today?
  • What does it cost to run, and what breaks at 10x users?
  • Which sponsor tool did you use, and where in the flow?
  • What would you build with one more month?

What loses points

  • Opening with names and colleges instead of the problem
  • Architecture slides before anyone has seen the product
  • Showing a feature that doesn't work yet
  • Buzzword stacks ("AI-powered blockchain platform")
  • Going over time. Many panels cut you off mid-sentence.
